About the Restore sleep tracker app

Sleep is precious — and you already know it.

We live in a world that constantly pressures us to sacrifice sleep for the next priority, the next deadline, the next trade-off. But sleep isn’t a luxury. It’s the foundation of how we think, decide, feel, and recover. Without it, everything else suffers.

Restore exists to help you understand your sleep — your patterns, your trends, your habits — and to gently guide you toward better ones. Whether you’re trying to fix a broken sleep schedule or simply curious about what’s going on while you rest, you’ll find the insight you need here.

Better sleep == Better you.

✨ Quick actions

The buttons you see are pegged to tonight’s sleep — not today’s calendar date. So if you’re logging at 1am, those actions still belong to the night you’re currently in, not yesterday. Think of it less like a clock and more like a session: it opens when you start winding down, and closes when you wake up.

Within that session, the app tries to meet you where you are:

  • 🌇
    Early evening You’ll typically see options to log bedtime and falling asleep.
  • 🧻
    Deep into the night Once you’re settled in, a bathroom break option appears.
  • 🌅
    Morning Waking up takes center stage.
  • ☀️
    During the day When you’re well into your wakeful hours, you’ll see the option to log a nap if your energy levels suggest you might need one.

You don’t need to tap anything in order. If you already logged that you’re in bed, that button quietly steps aside. The shortcuts are meant to reflect what’s actually likely next for you, so you’re not hunting through a list to find the relevant action.

You can bring it back in Settings.

Tonight target and guidance

On the Dashboard, Tonight can follow your recent average or saved targets for sleep, wake, or both. Below the bar, a small matrix shows, per pole, your rolling natural average, the guided time Tonight is using on the bar, your guidance pace (gentle, normal, or steady), and your saved target. When guidance is on, short callouts describe the phase (initiating, transitioning, locking in, on target) and whether the app is nudging you toward the goal or holding at the target in maintain mode. There is no hidden snap: the guided time moves only by your pace cap each evaluation, so closing the last few minutes can take more than one night on a gentle pace.

Drift is the slow slide you get when real life keeps nudging you later: you go to bed a little later, or wake a little later, night after night, and your rolling average creeps in the same direction. The gentle pace is meant mainly to hold off that drift—keeping you near your current habit, or changing only very gradually. With typical smoothing, think on the order of about an hour over roughly five weeks when you are deliberately closing a gap (not a straight-line promise; the window wiggles as nights drop in and out).

The steady pace is the fastest option: it allows up to 15 minutes per night toward a saved target, so moving your schedule on the order of about an hour over roughly two weeks is realistic when you need a quicker adjustment. That is often the more practical choice for deliberate schedule moves, but it is more at risk of not sticking for some people—your circadian rhythm does not reset on a tidy calendar grid, so brisker shifts can feel harder to lock in. Normal sits between gentle and steady.

A note on movement: Guided times don’t always move in a straight line toward your target. Because the schedule is built from a rolling average of your recent nights, a single new night only shifts that average by a fraction — and whatever night drops out of the window can tug it the other way. So you may see the guided time move earlier for a few days, then tick later again, even while the long-run trend is heading where you want. That’s a property of the smoothing, not a verdict on what’s biologically “best.” A gradual shift is often easier to stick with; if you need to move faster, switching toward steady tightens the timeline at the cost of a bumpier ride for your body clock.

What the icons mean on Tonight

  • 🧭
    Guided time On the knob row when that pole has a saved target and guidance is on (including maintain mode on target). Also labels the Guided and Pace columns in the matrix.
  • 🎯
    Saved target A marker above the bar for each pole where you have a saved goal and it still differs from the schedule Tonight is using. Tap the marker to clear that target. The Target column in the matrix shows the same saved time.
  • 🍃
    Recent average for that pole On the knob row when you have no saved target for that pole, or you have a target but guidance is off for that pole. Labels the Natural column in the matrix. You can have 🍃 on one pole and 🧭 on the other.

Remaining Wake Time

⚙️ To change those boundaries or phase heads-up timing, open Settings and use Remaining wake time.

Shows how much time is left in your day before your recommended sleep time. Open, Winding down, and Pre-sleep follow your percent of wake time remaining until average fell-asleep—meant to gently guide your pace, not rush you. A fourth phase, Sleep, is dynamic: it appears when you have logged bed or fell-asleep for the night and have not yet logged wake; it is not controlled by the percentage sliders.

The percentage-based phases feel like this:

  • ☀️
    Open time This is the primary portion of your day, with plenty of runway. The feeling is steady, normal, and no urgency.
  • 🌇
    Winding down It is not quite time to sleep, but avoid starting heavy or demanding activities. Caffiene is not recommended from this point.
  • 🛏️
    Pre-sleep Begin preparing for rest. Avoid meals and stimulating activity.
  • 🌙
    Sleep You have logged bed or fell-asleep; wake is not logged yet. The header shows this phase in the evening sleep window or overnight until you log get-up—not from your wake-time percentages.

Daily flags

How strong is a daily flag?

The flag color is how far that measure drifted from your previous seven logged nights. (Fewer than 7 prior nights in the log will show an insufficient-data message.)

😴 Asleep is only flagged when you fell asleep later than your baseline; falling asleep earlier is not flagged.

⌛ Duration uses the worse of two checks (not added together): shorter than your recent average, or absolute short sleep—under 6 hours (slight), under 5 hours (moderate), under 4 hours (severe)—based on total sleep including naps.

🌅 Wake compares earlier or later than the baseline.

👁️ WASO (wake after sleep onset) counts wake episodes longer than 20 minutes: 1 → slight, 2 → moderate, 3 or more → severe.

On the history calendar, the cell color is the worst of those severities among asleep, duration, and WASO.

Night notes (emoji labels)

From the Log screen you can tag a night with optional emoji “notes” (kids, pet, alcohol, travel, stress, work, caffeine, late meal, illness, loud/noise). They are saved with that night’s row, show next to daily flags on the timeline and dashboard, and do not change scores or charts.

Sleep statistics

The Statistics page summarizes your logged nights for a range you pick (past week through this year). You get side-by-side columns for all days, work days, and free days (weekends and holidays): averages for total sleep, bed and sleep times, delays, nap habits, mid-sleep, and earliest or latest bed, fell-asleep, and wake.

Social lag is a simple way to see whether your sleep timing shifts between workdays and days off. We compare the middle of your sleep on workdays with the middle on free days. A larger gap usually means your weekday routine and your weekend rhythm are not lining up—useful for noticing schedule strain, not a grade on how you sleep.

Wake lag compares how you wake on mornings when an alarm was involved versus mornings when you woke without one. If the number is positive, your natural wake time tends to run later than when the alarm pulls you up—so the alarm is getting you out of bed a bit earlier than you might on your own.
